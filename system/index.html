RALLY Hybrid Movement System

Movement codes
I2A5B4
DB Clean Step-Up

The goal is not to win every workout. The goal is to keep more useful capacity, cleaner movement, and better recovery for more years.

Operating model

Separate the pieces before you raise the dose.

Train daily, but classify stress first. Build movement quality, neural skill, strength, sprint speed, endurance, mobility, coordination, and recovery as one coherent system with scalable floors and hard caps.

daily not maximal

Daily Does Not Mean Maximal

Daily training works when low days stay low, restore days still count, and high days are deliberately concentrated.

capacity stack

Train Capacities, Not Random Modalities

Movement, neural skill, strength, speed, endurance, mobility, coordination, and recovery each get a clear job before exercises are mixed.

speed before fatigue

Speed Lives Before Fatigue

Sprints, jumps, throws, and high-skill practice happen fresh with full recovery and stop when the signal gets worse.

scale identity

Scale Dose, Preserve Identity

When readiness is low, reduce load, volume, density, range, or complexity before changing the session intent.

Coherent system

Eight capacities, one stress ledger.

RALLY treats hybrid training as a set of trainable capacities. A session names the capacity first, then picks movements, implements, and dose tags that serve that job.

Operating rule

Start with positions and timing, add force, add speed, add endurance, then integrate. If quality drops, scale the dose before changing the identity of the day.

Sprint architecture

Sprinting is high-skill strength, not conditioning.

True sprint work is a neural and tissue-quality exposure. It belongs before fatigue, with enough rest to make every rep fast, coordinated, and safe enough to repeat next week.

01 K3

Own The Joints

Ankles, hips, T-spine, shoulders, and trunk can move without pinching or compensation.

Use before every sprint block and on restore days.
02 KJ1

Prepare The Spring

Foot lock, ankle pulses, low contacts, and quiet landings before any hard acceleration.

Move to faster contacts only when rhythm stays relaxed.
03 J1

Elastic Contacts

Pogos and line hops build stiffness, tendon tolerance, rhythm, and lower-leg timing.

Stop while contacts still sound quiet and reactive.
04 J3/J4

Hops, Bounds, Landings

Single-leg catches, broad jumps, bounds, and deceleration teach force acceptance and projection.

Advance only when landings are owned, not collapsed.
05 J5

Acceleration

10-30 m starts with full walk-back or timed rest. Power comes before conditioning.

Add reps or distance only when splits and mechanics stay stable.
06 J6

Flying Sprint

Build-up into a short max-velocity zone to train speed reserve, upright mechanics, and rhythm.

Tiny dose. Stop at any guarded stride, heavy contact, or speed drop.
07 JF1

Speed Reserve Integration

Pair short speed first with easy aerobic or technical work second so speed raises the ceiling without becoming a metcon.

Never reverse the order when speed is the priority.

Sprint Rules

  1. Place true sprint work on high days after warm-up and before heavy lower-body lifting or hard conditioning.
  2. Use full recovery: most reps should feel like practice, not breathless intervals.
  3. Track a simple split, distance, contact count, or perceived mechanics so quality has a cap.
  4. Do not sprint hard after heavy mixed-modal fatigue, poor sleep, or a protective hamstring/calf signal.
  5. On low days, use drills, walks, skips, mobility, or relaxed strides instead of maximal sprinting.
Grease the groove

Grease the groove before you chase fatigue.

Use GTG as motor-learning practice: frequent clean inputs for position, timing, breathing, confidence, and tissue tolerance. The point is a better nervous-system signal, not a secret extra workout.

GTG Low fatigue. High frequency. Clean signal.

Keep it on low days, warm-up margins, or short practice windows. A set should make the next set better, not steal from tomorrow.

Stay submaximal

Use roughly 30-60% of a clean max, or a variation that feels like RPE 4-6.

Spread the reps

Run tiny sets across the day, in warm-ups, or between low-stress work blocks with generous rest.

Protect the signal

Stop every set with perfect speed, position, breathing, and 2-4 reps in reserve.

Graduate slowly

Add reps, range, load, or density only after the groove stays clean for a full week.
